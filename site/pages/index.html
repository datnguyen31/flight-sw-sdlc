An onboard housekeeping telemetry service for a satellite computer, built under a complete, traceable software life cycle following ECSS-E-ST-40C (software engineering) and ECSS-Q-ST-80C (product assurance). The code is deliberately small. The subject of the project is the process around it: requirements with their reasons, recorded design decisions, fault management as a planned increment, verification at three levels, and a report that states what it cannot claim.

What the service does

Operators know a spacecraft's health only through its telemetry. If the housekeeping stream stops, cannot be read, or cannot be placed in time, they are blind, and a fault can develop faster than the next ground contact. The service therefore does two jobs:

  • Reports health. It samples 32 parameters every second and, when ground has enabled reporting, sends a CCSDS space packet carrying a PUS Service 3 housekeeping report (TM[3,25]) every four seconds, with onboard CUC time and a CRC.
  • Watches health onboard. It checks every parameter against soft and hard limits. A condition confirmed over three consecutive samples is reported as a PUS Service 5 event, and a critical one puts the unit in safe mode until ground clears it.

It runs as two static RTEMS tasks on RTEMS 7 (BSP sparc/leon3), executed on the Renode emulator's LEON3 platform.
